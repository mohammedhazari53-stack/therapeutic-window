**The news.** On September 17, the FDA granted full approval to FAYUVI (rebisufligene etisparvovec-hopf), Ultragenyx's gene therapy previously known as UX111. It treats the neurologic manifestations of mucopolysaccharidosis type IIIA (MPS IIIA, or Sanfilippo syndrome type A) in children with preserved neurodevelopmental function. It is the first approved treatment for the disease, and the decision came two days ahead of the September 19 PDUFA date.

## The science in one paragraph

MPS IIIA is caused by a missing lysosomal enzyme, so heparan sulfate (HS) builds up in the brain and causes progressive, ultimately fatal neurodegeneration in childhood. FAYUVI is a single intravenous dose of an AAV9 vector that delivers a working copy of the gene. AAV9 was chosen because it can cross into the central nervous system. The biomarker story is simple: if the enzyme is working, HS levels in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) should fall.

## The data behind the approval

- **Design:** the Transpher A study plus long-term follow-up, with data extending to about eight years. The comparison was against an **external natural history cohort**, not a randomized control arm, which is common in ultra-rare pediatric disease.
- **Size:** 17 treated patients (modified intention-to-treat) versus 27 untreated natural-history patients.
- **Biomarker:** reductions in CSF heparan sulfate across all age groups.
- **Clinical outcome:** on the Bayley-III cognitive raw score, measured from 24 to 60 months of age, treated children scored **23.5 points higher** than the natural history cohort (p<0.0001).

## Safety

The most common adverse reactions were elevated liver enzymes (85%), vomiting (67%), abnormal behavior (56%), diarrhea (48%) and fever (41%). The label includes warnings for hepatotoxicity, thrombocytopenia and thrombotic microangiopathy. These are familiar risks for high-dose systemic AAV and will require monitoring.

## Why it matters

**The CRL was about manufacturing, not the medicine.** The FDA rejected the first application in July 2025. Ultragenyx said the letter cited chemistry, manufacturing and controls information and observations from facility inspections. The resubmission fixed those issues, and the approval followed about 14 months later. FAYUVI is made entirely in the U.S., at Ultragenyx's Bedford, Massachusetts facility and at Andelyn Biosciences in Columbus, Ohio.

**The label rewards early diagnosis.** Restricting it to children with *preserved* neurodevelopment means the commercial opportunity depends on finding patients before they decline. Watch for newborn-screening and diagnostic efforts to follow.

**There's a voucher.** Ultragenyx received a rare pediatric disease priority review voucher with the approval. Those vouchers have historically sold for nine-figure sums, which is non-dilutive capital if the company chooses to sell.

## What I'm watching

1. **Launch speed.** Ultragenyx expects to ship to qualified treatment centers within 30–60 days.
2. **Price.** It hasn't been disclosed yet, so watch how it compares with other one-time gene therapies.
3. **Whether the voucher is sold,** and for how much.
