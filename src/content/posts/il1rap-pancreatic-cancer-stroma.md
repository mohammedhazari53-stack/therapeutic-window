**The study.** Researchers at the University of Miami's Sylvester Comprehensive Cancer Center, led by surgical oncologist Jashodeep Datta, M.D., report in *JCI Insight* that IL1RAP helps explain why pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) resists chemotherapy and immunotherapy, and that blocking it can loosen that resistance in mice.

## The problem it's trying to solve

PDAC is surrounded by dense, fibrotic stroma and suppressive myeloid cells. Together they keep T cells out and blunt both chemotherapy and checkpoint inhibitors. Most "stroma-busting" strategies have failed in the clinic, so the field needs targets that act on several parts of that network at once.

## What they did

The team took a deliberately translational path, starting with patient data and then moving into mice:

- **Clinical transcriptomics:** a post hoc analysis of tumors from the COMPASS trial linked high IL1RAP expression across immune, stromal and tumor cells to chemotherapy resistance.
- **Single-cell atlas:** they mapped IL1RAP across **654,997 cells from 79 patients** in the Human Tumor Atlas Network PDAC dataset.
- **Mouse model:** they treated the autochthonous PKT model with *mNadu*, a mouse analog of **nadunolimab**, Cantargia's clinical-stage anti-IL1RAP antibody.
- **Human biopsies:** they examined paired tumor samples from the CANFOUR trial of nadunolimab.

## What they found

- Anti-IL1RAP alone **slowed tumor growth** (P = 0.019) and **reduced stromal fibrosis**.
- It **decreased CD11b⁺ myeloid cells** and **increased CD3⁺ T-cell infiltration**, expanding memory-progenitor-exhausted T cells (the population that tends to respond to anti-PD-1).
- A four-drug regimen of anti-IL1RAP plus gemcitabine/paclitaxel plus anti-PD-1 **extended median survival to 54 days**, versus 43 days for chemoimmunotherapy alone or for the control antibody (P < 0.001).

## Why it matters

**It targets a network, not a single cell type.** IL1RAP is a co-receptor used by several IL-1 family signals, so one antibody can hit tumor cells, fibroblasts and myeloid cells together. That's the multi-compartment effect PDAC seems to need.

**The drug already exists.** Because the mouse work used an analog of a clinical-stage antibody, and the human biopsies came from an existing trial, the path to patients is shorter than for most preclinical findings.

**Keep the effect size in perspective.** A gain of 11 days in median survival in an aggressive mouse model is statistically strong, but it is not a cure, and mouse survival gains often shrink in humans. The mechanistic data (less fibrosis, more T cells) are the more encouraging part.

## What's next

Sylvester plans a **neoadjuvant trial** that adds IL1RAP-targeted therapy to chemoimmunotherapy in patients with operable pancreatic cancer *before* surgery. Treating before surgery means the resected tumor can be compared with the pre-treatment biopsy, which is a direct test of whether the microenvironment actually changes in people. The work is supported by a four-year, $800,000 V Foundation translational research grant.
