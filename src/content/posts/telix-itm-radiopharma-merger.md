**The deal.** Australia's Telix Pharmaceuticals has agreed to acquire ITM Isotope Technologies Munich, a privately held German radiopharmaceutical company, for $1.65 billion upfront. Up to $700 million more depends on how ITM's lead drug performs with regulators and in the market.

**The backdrop.** On August 7, the FDA sent ITM a complete response letter (a rejection, pending fixes) for ITM-11 (¹⁷⁷Lu-edotreotide), its radioligand therapy for gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (GEP-NETs). According to ITM, the letter raised no concerns about the clinical data, nonclinical data, or safety. The problems were chemistry, manufacturing and controls (CMC) and a third-party commercial facility.

## How the price is built

The headline number is on a cash-free, debt-free basis, so it helps to break it down:

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| New Telix shares to ITM holders (105.8M at US$11.841) | ~$1.25B |
| ITM net debt assumed | ~$302M |
| Management rollover and transaction costs | ~$96M |
| **Upfront total** | **~$1.65B** |

Then the contingent piece, all of it tied to ITM-11:

- **Up to $250M in regulatory milestones:** $100M for FDA approval in G1–G2 GEP-NETs (target by end of 2027), $100M for G2–G3 GEP-NETs (by end of 2030), and $50M for lung NETs (by end of 2031).
- **Up to $450M in a sales milestone:** triggered if ITM-11 global net sales exceed $150M in FY2030.

After closing, Telix shareholders would own about 76.3% of the combined company and ITM's shareholders about 23.7%.

## Why it matters

**The CRL was priced in, not ignored.** Because the first $100M milestone depends on FDA approval by the end of 2027, ITM's sellers still carry much of the regulatory risk. And because they're paid mostly in Telix stock, they also share in whatever the combined company becomes.

**Supply is the strategic asset.** ITM says it is the only producer of commercial-grade lutetium-177 at global scale. It also makes actinium-225 and terbium-161, and it has 15 years of exclusive access to Bruce Power's nuclear reactors in Canada. In radiopharma, the isotope supply chain *is* the moat. Novartis has dominated the market since launching Lutathera (2018) and Pluvicto (2022), and this deal gives Telix vertical integration from reactor to patient.

**The combined revenue base is real.** ITM reported $273M in 2025 revenue, and the two companies project pro forma 2026 revenue above $1.3B. That makes this more than a pipeline bet.

## What I'm watching

1. **ITM-11 resubmission timing.** The $100M milestone clock runs to December 31, 2027. The COMPOSE expansion study also has an interim analysis expected in the first half of 2027.
2. **The Telix shareholder vote**, expected in November 2026, with closing targeted by the end of FY2026.
3. **Whether the CMC fix sits inside ITM's own plants or at the third party.** That tells you how much control Telix really gains.
